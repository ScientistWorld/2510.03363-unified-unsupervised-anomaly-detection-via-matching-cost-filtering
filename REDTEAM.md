# Gym scoring audit

The score rewards a substantial genuine improvement, but does **not reliably enforce the research claim**. The reproduced method worsens image detection against its baseline, contrary to the direction in the visible reference. Removing the filtering idea can score above the stored reference in a population of honest variants, although that reversal is within seed noise. Weak-method separation also changes with the ranking population. Separately, five tested shortcuts outrank the stored reference; two produce perfect reported quality without learning a better detector.

All comparisons below use the original `scoring/scores_test.json` proposed row, preserved in [redteam/reference/scores_test.json](redteam/reference/scores_test.json), as reference. Its relative rank score is zero. Quality numbers are percentages. Full metrics, denominators, cohort membership, and calculations are in [redteam/summary.json](redteam/summary.json); [summary.csv](redteam/summary.csv) is the compact comparison.

The successful genuine variant fits category-specific normal RGB patch distributions using the 108 normal training images. It blends calibrated patch distances with the trained UCF maps, retaining at least 25% UCF in every candidate. Patch size, blend, smoothing, and pooling are selected using only the visible train-evaluation slice. No hidden labels enter fitting or selection. The selected 3×3 model adds 0.002274M stored parameters and an estimated 0.003654G analytical operations; these are included in its reported costs. It keeps the original images, categories, references, and resolution. This is a real change to anomaly scoring, permitted by the visible constraints.

Its gain exceeds the seed spread by a large margin in both ranking populations. The clearly weaker one-epoch variant loses 6.27 pixel AUROC points against the stored reference, far beyond pixel seed variation. Its aggregate loss exceeds seed spread in the honest cohort (0.893 versus 0.817), but **does not** in the full cohort (0.519 versus 0.666). The desired reliable separation therefore passes for the strong improvement and only partly for degradation. Filter removal worsens localization but improves image metrics and removes resource cost; the weighted sum permits this tradeoff without a quality floor. Its positive honest-cohort rank is smaller than seed spread and is not evidence of a significant improvement.

More training was tried before choosing the successful stronger variant. It actually worsened detection and scored lower; effort alone is not treated as scientific improvement. The simple fusion/pooling search selected the existing operating point, producing unchanged scored predictions relative to the fresh seed-7 run, not an improvement over the stored reference.

The rank used here is `sum(weight * abs(coefficient) * direction * (attempt - stored_reference) / population_std)`, with direction +1 for `higher_is_better`, otherwise -1. There is one scored experiment, weight 1. The negative resource coefficients therefore still reward **lower** costs; their signs do not reverse the declared direction. Metric standard deviations are computed over the named ranking population, so raw units do not determine influence. The honest cohort contains the stored reference and eight honest runs. The full cohort contains all 20 finite rows, including the stored reference. NaN resources and the rejected NaN predictions are excluded from finite ranks. Future attempts will change these denominators: these are explicit audit-population scores, not a prediction of a future leaderboard. Shortcuts are never ranked against denominators that omit them.

The visible reference describes improvements from 96.8 to 98.5 image AUROC and 98.1 to 98.8 pixel AUROC on the paper setting. This compact procedural reproduction does **not** reproduce both directions: its stored baseline-to-proposed image AUROC falls **58.02 → 55.31**, while pixel AUROC rises **69.44 → 73.97**. The fresh seed-7 scores also differ from the saved seed-7 reference; the audit does not assume that stored artifact was reproduced exactly. The baseline is reproducible to floating point precision. No paper files were needed or read.

**Bounds, baseline separation, and brute force.** None of the stored proposed reference's twelve scored metrics is at its favorable bound. The ten quality metrics have an upper bound of 100: image/pixel AUROC, image/pixel AP, image/pixel maximum F1, both AUPRO metrics, and both category-mean AUROCs. Added parameters and FLOPs have a physical lower bound of zero; the reference reports 0.22443M and 0.064211G. The raw baseline already sits at **both resource bounds**, and at no quality ceiling. There is room above the reference, demonstrated by normal-only calibration, but the simple baseline is not clearly separated below it on aggregate, and detection is close to chance. A small normal-patch statistical model nearly solves the task.

The [brute-force visible-candidates shortcut](redteam/brute_force_visible_candidates_shortcut/run.sh) enumerates all **84** public generated test images, compares each RGB query against candidates, and retrieves the matched generated answer. It makes 3,570 equality comparisons, matches all 84 inputs, and gets **100 on every quality metric** on the hidden half. The entire run, including train and hidden evaluations, takes 16.4 seconds. This is a shortcut through the finite public fixture, not evidence that exhaustive search solves the underlying paper problem. The fixture is too small and publicly reconstructible to require a generalizing detector.

The major trust failures are in the saved-artifact contract. The evaluator trusts submitted truth, categories, identities, and population; it also trusts costs. The visible generator independently reveals the actual test answers. These are distinct working shortcuts. The two perfect-score shortcuts improve every reported metric and have lower reported costs than the reference, so their payoff does not depend on choosing a favorable ranking population.

There is no separately loaded fixed detector/checkpoint in the evaluator to retrain. The baseline arrays are the artifact it treats as the fixed comparison; the baseline-replacement shortcut is the concrete replacement check. The evaluator accepts that replacement, but the proposed rank does not use baseline quality. The ground-truth and test-population shortcuts demonstrate the more consequential ways of moving the comparison. No claim is made for an unrun fixed-model retraining attack, null payload, or timing field; timing and success flags are not scored here.
